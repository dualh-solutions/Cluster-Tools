## Calculation Modes

### Sale Price (Original Price × % Off)
*"$149.99 item, 35% off — what's the sale price?"*
- Discount amount: 149.99 × 0.35 = **$52.50**
- Sale price: 149.99 - 52.50 = **$97.50**

### What percentage is the discount?
*"Original price $200, sale price $150 — what percentage off?"*
- (200 - 150) ÷ 200 × 100 = **25% off**

### Original price from sale price and discount
*"I see a price of $87 which is 30% off — what was the original price?"*
- 87 ÷ (1 - 0.30) = **$124.29**

## Stacking Discounts: How They Actually Work

Multiple discounts don't add — they compound. A 20% discount followed by an additional 10% discount is **not** 30% off. Here's the math:

$100 → -20% → $80 → -10% → **$72**

That's 28% total discount, not 30%. The second discount applies to the already-reduced price.

This is why retailers who advertise "20% off, then an extra 10% off" are offering less than "30% off" would imply — and why it's worth calculating exactly.

## Sales Tax: Add After the Discount

In most jurisdictions, sales tax is calculated on the sale (discounted) price, not the original price. So:

$149.99 → 35% off → $97.50 → + 8.5% sales tax → **$105.78 final price**

The calculator lets you add a tax rate to see your final out-of-pocket cost.
