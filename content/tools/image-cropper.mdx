## Crop Images to Any Size — Without Leaving Your Browser

Cropping removes unwanted areas from an image to focus on the subject, meet a specific aspect ratio requirement, or reduce file dimensions. Cluster Tools's Image Cropper gives you a drag-and-select interface that runs entirely in your browser — no upload, no account, no waiting.

## Crop Modes

### Free Crop
Drag a selection handle to any shape. Useful when you know exactly what area you want to keep and don't need to meet a specific ratio.

### Aspect Ratio Crop
Lock the crop selection to a specific ratio. The selection resizes proportionally as you drag. Common presets include:
- **1:1** — square (Instagram posts, profile photos, app icons)
- **16:9** — widescreen (YouTube thumbnails, social sharing, desktop wallpapers)
- **4:3** — traditional (many older camera sensors, presentation slides)
- **3:2** — DSLR standard (the native ratio of most full-frame camera sensors)
- **9:16** — vertical/portrait (Instagram Reels, TikTok, phone wallpapers)

### Pixel-Perfect Crop
Specify the exact output dimensions in pixels. The crop selection automatically sizes to match, and you drag to position it over the desired area.

## Common Cropping Use Cases

**Removing unwanted backgrounds or borders** — scanned documents often have dark borders or shadows at the edges from the scanner lid. Cropping removes these cleanly.

**Creating social media thumbnails** — YouTube requires 1280×720px thumbnails (16:9). Cropping a longer horizontal photo to 16:9 before uploading gives you control over exactly which part of the image appears.

**Profile photos** — most platforms display profile photos as circles or squares. Cropping your photo to 1:1 before uploading ensures the face is centered, not cut off by the platform's automatic cropping.

**Product photo cleanup** — e-commerce product photos often need tight cropping to remove excess white space around the product and meet platform dimension requirements.

**Straightening and re-composing shots** — cropping is the simplest form of photo composition adjustment. Moving the subject off-center (rule of thirds) or removing distracting elements at the edge of the frame.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Does cropping reduce image quality?**
No. Cropping is a non-destructive operation on the pixel data — it simply selects a subset of pixels and discards the rest. The kept pixels are identical to the originals. Quality is only affected if you also compress or re-encode the output.

**Can I undo a crop?**
Yes — re-upload the original image (or use the reset button) and crop again. The tool doesn't modify your original file.

**What image formats are supported?**
JPG, PNG, and WebP. The output format matches the input format.

**Can I crop images with transparent backgrounds (PNG)?**
Yes — transparency in PNG files is preserved correctly after cropping.

**Is there a minimum crop size?**
You can crop down to a single pixel in theory, though the browser won't let you make a selection smaller than a few pixels in the UI. For practical use, the minimum sensible crop is around 10×10px.
