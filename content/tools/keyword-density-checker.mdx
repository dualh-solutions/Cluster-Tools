## Analyze Keyword Density — Understand Your Content's Topical Focus

Keyword density is the percentage of times a keyword appears in a text relative to the total word count. It's a basic but useful metric for understanding which terms dominate your content.

Cluster Tools's Keyword Density Checker analyzes your text entirely in your browser. No content is sent to any server.

## What Keyword Density Tells You (And What It Doesn't)

**What it tells you:**
- Which terms appear most frequently in your text.
- Whether a specific keyword appears at a natural frequency or is over-represented.
- Whether related terms you'd expect to see are present.

**What it doesn't tell you:**
- Whether your content is well-ranked for a keyword.
- Whether Google considers the keyword relevant to the page.
- Exact "ideal" keyword density percentages.

### The "Ideal" Keyword Density Myth

You may have encountered advice like "keyword density should be 2–3%." This is outdated thinking from the early 2000s. Modern Google uses natural language understanding (BERT, MUM) to evaluate topical relevance, not keyword counting. **There is no optimal keyword density to target.**

What matters is **topical completeness**: covering a topic thoroughly with natural language, using synonyms, related entities, and answering the questions a reader would have. Keyword density checking is useful for catching obvious over-optimization (stuffing) or identifying gaps in coverage — not for hitting a numerical target.

## How Keyword Density Is Calculated

`Density = (keyword_occurrences / total_words) × 100`

For a 1,000-word article where "PDF compression" appears 5 times:
`Density = (5 / 1000) × 100 = 0.5%`

The checker also shows absolute occurrence count, which is often more useful than the percentage for short texts.

## Practical Use Cases

**Checking for keyword stuffing** — if your target keyword appears at 5%+ density, it's likely affecting readability and could trigger Google's over-optimization filters.

**Content gap analysis** — a luxury hotel article that never mentions "amenities," "check-in," or "location" may be missing topically relevant terms.

**Competitive analysis** — paste a competitor's top-ranking article to see which terms they emphasize and at what density.

**Proofreading for over-repetition** — even without an SEO goal, writing quality improves when no single word dominates excessively.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What keyword density percentage is too high?**
No specific percentage is harmful on its own. Unnaturally high density — a term appearing every other sentence in an obviously forced way — affects readability and signals keyword stuffing. 1–2% is common for naturally-written content about a specific topic; higher than 3–4% usually indicates over-optimization.

**Does Google penalize for keyword density?**
Not with a specific threshold. Google's systems detect unnatural writing patterns more broadly. Natural writing that covers a topic thoroughly naturally produces keyword density within acceptable ranges.

**Should I optimize for single keywords or phrases?**
Focus on phrases and semantic clusters, not individual keywords. Modern search intent is satisfied by comprehensive coverage of a topic, not by hitting keyword frequency targets.
