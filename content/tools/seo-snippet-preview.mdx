## Preview Your Google Search Result — Before Publishing

The SERP (Search Engine Results Page) snippet is what users see before they visit your site. It's the first impression: title, URL, and description — all in about 2 seconds of attention. Cluster Tools's SEO Snippet Preview shows you exactly how your page will appear in Google desktop and mobile search results, including accurate character-limit visualizations.

## What Makes a Strong SERP Snippet

### The Title
Google shows approximately 580px worth of title text on desktop (roughly 55–60 characters for standard font), and truncates with `...` beyond that. A truncated title loses its CTA and may confuse users about what the page contains.

**Good title anatomy:** `Primary Keyword — Modifier | Brand`

Example: `Compress PDF Online — Free & Private | Cluster Tools`

The primary keyword leads, a differentiating modifier adds context, and the brand name at the end builds recognition without consuming valuable keyword space.

### The URL
Google typically shows the domain and breadcrumb path (e.g., `clustertools.online › tools › pdf › compress-pdf`). Clean, hierarchical URL structure enhances trustworthiness and topical relevance signals.

### The Meta Description
Not a ranking signal, but the key determinant of **click-through rate**. Google may bold words in the description that match the user's search query, making them visually salient.

A strong description: answers "what is this page about and why should I click?" in 155–160 characters.

### Rich Results (Structured Data)
Certain pages earn enhanced snippet formats from structured data:
- **Star ratings** — from `Review`/`AggregateRating` schema.
- **FAQ dropdowns** — from `FAQPage` schema.
- **Sitelinks** — from site popularity and structure.
- **How-to steps** — from `HowTo` schema.

The Cluster Tools preview shows the standard snippet; rich results require validation in [Google's Rich Results Test](https://search.google.com/test/rich-results).

## Desktop vs. Mobile Snippet Differences

Google's desktop and mobile SERPs render snippets with slightly different layouts:

- **Desktop** — ~580px title display, ~920px description display.
- **Mobile** — slightly narrower, titles may truncate slightly earlier. URLs are often formatted as breadcrumbs on mobile.

The preview tool shows both viewports so you can optimize for both.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Will Google always use my title and description?**
No. Google rewrites titles in roughly 60–70% of cases and descriptions similarly. Google prefers your title when it's accurate, concise, and well-formatted. It tends to rewrite when the title is truncated, keyword-stuffed, or doesn't match the page's actual content.

**Does the snippet affect my ranking?**
The snippet itself isn't a direct ranking signal. But click-through rate (CTR) — how often people click your result — is a behavioral signal that Google uses as a ranking factor. Better CTR → more clicks → potentially improved rankings.

**Should desktop and mobile descriptions be different?**
You can only set one meta description. Optimize it for the character limit that produces the most informative visible snippet on mobile (the more restrictive viewport).
