## Convert WebP to JPG — Maximum Compatibility

WebP is the superior web image format, but not every platform, app, or tool supports it yet. Older versions of Photoshop, some email clients, certain CMS platforms, and Windows applications may struggle to open WebP files. Converting to JPG solves the compatibility problem instantly.

Cluster Tools converts WebP to JPG entirely in your browser using the browser's native decoding API. Your images never leave your device.

## When You Need WebP to JPG Conversion

**Opening WebP images downloaded from the web** — Chrome and other modern browsers save images in WebP format when you right-click and save. If you then try to open them in Windows Photo Viewer, older Lightroom versions, or upload to a platform that doesn't accept WebP, you need a JPG version.

**Sharing with people who use older software** — while browser support for WebP is universal, desktop application support isn't. Converting to JPG ensures anyone can open and view the image regardless of their software.

**CMS and platform uploads** — some content management systems, e-commerce platforms, and social media networks still only accept JPG and PNG uploads.

**Printing services** — photo printing labs typically accept JPG (and sometimes PNG/TIFF), but rarely WebP.

**Email clients** — while modern webmail (Gmail, Outlook.com) displays WebP, some mail clients and corporate email systems may render WebP as a broken image.

## What Gets Lost in WebP to JPG Conversion

Be aware of a few things before converting:

- **Transparency is lost** — JPG doesn't support an alpha channel. Any transparent areas in the WebP become white (or your chosen background color) in the JPG.
- **Quality is fixed** — converting from WebP lossy to JPG introduces a second round of lossy compression. The quality will be slightly lower than the original WebP source. If you have access to the original high-quality source image, it's better to convert from that.
- **Animation is lost** — animated WebP files are exported as single-frame JPGs (the first frame). Static frames only.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Why did someone send me a WebP file?**
Modern browsers (Chrome, Edge, Firefox) often download images from websites in WebP format because websites increasingly use WebP for its smaller file sizes. The browser saved it in the format the website served it in.

**Is there quality loss when converting WebP to JPG?**
If the source WebP was lossy-compressed, then yes — converting to JPG applies another round of lossy compression. At a quality setting of 90%+, the additional degradation is minimal and not visible under normal viewing conditions.

**Can I convert multiple WebP files at once?**
Yes — batch conversion is supported. Drop multiple WebP files and download a ZIP of the JPG results.

**What if my WebP has a transparent background?**
The transparent areas will be filled with white in the JPG. If you need to preserve transparency, use [WebP to PNG](/tools/image/webp-to-jpg) — note that Cluster Tools currently exports to JPG; for transparency preservation you'd need to keep the WebP or use a PNG workflow.
